Method

What the board looks at, what it throws away, and how it scores what's left. The thresholds below are read live from the published board, so this page can't drift out of date with the code.

Sources

Polymarket leaderboards — top wallets by realized PnL across all-time, monthly and weekly windows, in four categories. A wallet qualifies on realized PnL and an ROI floor, which excludes churny market-makers who move size without making money.

Polymarket large-trade feed — recent taker buys above a dollar floor, kept only when the buyer is one of those wallets. This is the single source that produces tickets today.

Kalshi — built and runnable as an independent price on the same events, but deliberately not feeding the board. Matching markets by title produced confident false edges (Kalshi's "who will run for the nomination" against Polymarket's "will X win it" is a 48¢ difference that is simply correct). After guarding against those, genuine disagreements among liquid markets came to zero.

Research-only sources

These print on the Research page and produce no tickets. They have no settled-trade record yet, and a source with no record does not get to size a position.

Logical consistency — constraints the book must satisfy whoever is right about the world: a higher threshold cannot cost more than a lower one, an exclusive set cannot sum away from 1.00, and a harder event cannot outprice its own prerequisite. Being right about the future is not a prerequisite for collecting, which makes it the one family here that needs no forecast. Both legs must be quoted and fillable or the group is thrown away rather than assumed.

Crypto risk-neutral density — BTC and ETH thresholds priced off Deribit's free option chain instead of realized volatility, which is a different object from the distribution a threshold market actually pays on. Every crypto estimate is labelled with the model that produced it; a Deribit miss on BTC/ETH skips the market rather than quietly falling back to the weaker model.

CPI vs the Cleveland Fed nowcast — bracket prices against a normal distribution centred on the free daily nowcast. Its width is an assumption, floored at the nowcast's own measured error against 154 printed vintages, because a too-narrow one manufactures edge in every centre bracket. When that width disagrees with the market's own, the page says so — one disagreement about variance otherwise wears the costume of a dozen independent edges. The Fed-funds leg is absent: no free, terms-compliant source for the strip was found, and it fails closed rather than ship a scrape.

Filters

Every signal must survive all of these. Most don't — and the rejection counts are shown on the board so an empty result is explained rather than mysterious.

Conviction

A weighted blend, deliberately simple enough to check by hand. It ranks ideas against each other — it is not a probability of winning.

ComponentWeightMaxes out at
Wallet agreement30%3 wallets on the same side
Size behind it25%~$200,000
Freshness15%decays over 3 hours
Price drift20%zero at 6¢ past their entry
Time to resolve10%72 hours

Most live signals come from a single wallet, which caps the first term at a third of its weight — so a lone-wallet ticket rarely scores above about 0.80.

A bar, not a quota. The board used to publish the day's top eight. That answers "how many fit on the page", which is not a fact about any trade: it bought the eighth-best idea on a quiet day and skipped a better one on a busy day. Now every ticket above the conviction bar is published and none below it is, however many that turns out to be.

Where the bar sits is currently a guess, and it is meant to stop being one. Nothing in the blend above was fitted to a result — the weights were chosen, not learned. So tickets in a band below the bar are recorded by the paper trial without being recommended. Watching only the trades you would have taken tells you nothing about the ones you skipped, and a cutoff you never observe the far side of can never be shown to be wrong.

Sizing

House limits, applied against your current account value: every contract must cost at least $0.01, with a cap of 19 contracts per market and 38 per event, per $1,000 of account. On top of that the board risks 1% of the account per idea, scaled by conviction.

Size is frozen into the journal the moment you mark a trade placed, so later capital corrections never rewrite what a past trade cost.

Why it rejects so much

On 26 June the earlier, automated version placed four live orders. All four were on football matches already in their second half, and two of them were the same opinion twice — YES on one team and NO on the other, in both games.

Whales react to a goal faster than any polling loop, so those fills came at the post-event price with the information already gone. The in-play filter, the resolution window, the drift ceiling and the one-ticket-per-event rule all exist because of that afternoon, and replaying those four signals through today's filters produces nothing.

What this is not

It is not a bot, and it has no execution path — the code that once placed orders now refuses to construct, and going live again would be a separate decision behind its own deploy gate. It is not financial advice, and the conviction score is uncalibrated until the journal has enough settled trades to test it. Bankroll here is small enough that this phase is about proving process, not income.
